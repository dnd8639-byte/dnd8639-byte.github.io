David DeConti

I build systems for testing trading ideas honestly, and I keep a public record of every test I run, including the ones that fail.

Most trading strategies look profitable in a backtest and fail on new data. My work is about telling the difference: writing the plan down before running the test, counting every attempt, checking the tester on fake data where the answer is known, and publishing negative results alongside positive ones.

  • 2026Open source · Python

    mcpt-lab: a permutation-testing platform for trading strategies

    A Python library that tests whether a strategy beats the same tuning process run on hundreds of shuffled markets. I used it to replicate three published strategies, and then to measure how ten famous academic strategies performed after they were published.

    12 strategy tests · 1 weak survivor · famous strategies kept 30% of their edge after publication, and none kept a reliable profit after costs
